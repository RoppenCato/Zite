# The Maple guide: how to write a song with MapleStory inspiration

Written 2026-10-14 from the fourteen studies in `docs/studies/` (machine transcriptions of Robin's inspiration tracks,
so counts are approximate and instruments are guesses) and from what Robin has said about the studio's own pieces so
far. This is a working ruleset: section 7 is the feedback log, and every rule below is to be tweaked as Robin tells us
what he likes and does not like. Nothing from the studied pieces is copied; the rules are about how they are built.

## 2. Form

- **Length:** 64 to 100 bars at the chosen tempo, two to three minutes a loop. Forty bars was v1's length and it repeats
  every eighty seconds; the studies never loop that fast. A long loop is the first defence against tiring.
- **Open by saying the first phrase twice** (four bars, then the same four varied), over the thinnest arrangement the
  piece will have. Several pieces open on one chord and its sus for eight or sixteen bars (Lith Harbor's D and Dsus).
- **Bring the parts in one at a time.** Ellinia's bass arrives at bar 17, Orbis's bass grows from 8 notes per eight bars
  to 45; Ellinia Forest never has one. What arrives is an event; what is there from bar one is wallpaper.
- **Build by density**, not by volume (more notes in the middle voice, the lead doubled up an octave, the bass busier).
  The studies build to about two thirds (Ellinia: 120 to 288 notes per eight bars at bars 65 to 72 of 93); **Robin wants
  the full arrangement within the first sixteen bars** (feedback, 2026-10-14), so the build is short and the one big
  event is the breakdown and the return.
- **Then a breakdown**: the lead stops or nearly, the bass and middle carry on, one chord may be held (Ludibrium's sixteen
  bars on Asus; Login Screen's eight bars with two lead notes), or a real silence (Path of Time's five empty bars).
- **Bring the hook back late and varied**, after forty bars or more, as the thing the ear had almost forgotten
  (Ellinia at 41, Login at 121, Florina's I' at 73). Not every eight bars.
- **End with a short tail** of two to four bars on the tonic (held, or a sus resolving) so the loop's return to the thin
  opening is a breath. Slow pieces may instead rise to the end and let the loop itself be the drop (El Nath, Aqua Road).

## 3. Harmony

- **Sevenths on everything.** I and IV as maj7 (or 6), ii, iii, vi as m7, V as a dominant seventh. Plain triads are the
  exception (Lith Harbor's pedal, Ludibrium's D), used where the piece wants to be plain.
- **Loops of two to four chords**, two bars each, repeated for sixteen or thirty-two bars before anything changes:
  Ellinia vi IV ii I; Orbis I ii; Sleepywood I VI7; Nautilus IVsus V7; Aquarium I VI7 I vi then V IV V IV.
- **Sus4 before a cadence**: the V (or the I) as a sus for a bar or two at the end of an eight, then resolved. Ludibrium
  holds the sus for four bars; it is the Maple sound of waiting.
- **Bar one need not be the tonic.** Ellinia starts on V, then vi; Nautilus never rests on I. Starting on vi or IV keeps the
  piece floating; starting on I (Lith, Ludibrium, Aquarium, Orbis) grounds it. Choose by place.
- **One borrowed chord per piece**, once or twice: bVI and bVII (Lith's Bb6, C6), a maj7 a half step up (El Nath's
  Bmaj7), the minor's colours inside the major (Path of Time's Cm7, Eb, Bb; Aqua Road's Dmaj7 in E). One moment of
  strangeness, then home. Secondary dominants (V of ii, V of V) are the everyday colour (Florina, Ellinia Forest, Aquarium).
- **No key change** in the bright pieces studied; the section contrast is a chord loop that changes (Sleepywood's middle
  on IV and its dominant), not a new key. (v1's B section up a tone is our own idea; Robin liked v1, so it stays there.)

## 4. Melody

- **One rhythmic cell is the identity**: Ellinia's dotted eighth and sixteenth, Lith's figure in the middle voice. Repeat
  it with new pitches rather than inventing new rhythms. Repeated notes are welcome.
- **Stepwise inside a fifth**, chord tones on the strong beats, a leap at the phrase end. The lead sits between E4 and
  C6; it climbs an octave for lift (Ellinia Forest to G7, Ellinia's doubling at bars 17 to 32).
- **Lift by doubling, not by a new tune**: the same line an octave up in a second voice when a section should rise.
- **The lead rests.** In most pieces it is the smallest part (Lith 577 lead notes against 1590 middle; Sleepywood 241
  against 728). It speaks, stops, and the middle voice answers in the gap. Florina: 4.8 notes a second and a full tune.
- **Call and answer** between the lead and the middle voice, phrase by phrase, is what fills the bars between the hook's
  appearances without a second tune.

## 5. Arrangement

- **Three registers, each with a job.** Bass below E3: roots on beat one, octave jumps, eighths repeating the root, the
  fifth now and then, little walking (Ellinia, Lith, Nautilus). Middle E3 to E4: arpeggios and comping, the busiest part,
  the engine of the piece. Lead E4 and up: the tune, sparse.
- **The middle voice is where the energy is.** When a section should feel fuller, add middle-voice notes (a comp on the
  off-beats, a second arpeggio figure), not a louder lead.
- **Percussion light or none.** The transcriptions do not hear drums; what Robin has heard and liked (v1) had a shaker
  and a rim at low level. Keep them light, drop them in the breakdown, never a kick in a bright place.
- **Voices**: the game's GM palette as the studio builds them (`docs/sound-direction.md` section 1): flute or bells on
  the lead, piano and harp in the middle, strings as the pad, an FM bass. One lead at a time.

## 7. Robin's feedback, and what it changed (keep adding)

- **2026-10-14, v1 of the meadow (the song, 40 bars, all voices, shaker and rim, a hook that returns every 8 bars):** "this
  music is so much better" (than the game's version). Kept as an option. Rule: the full arrangement with real voices is
  the baseline; the hook, the flute and the bells are liked.
- **2026-10-14, the danger layer:** dropped. "The music will just be what it is." Rule: one mix, no layers that react to
  the game.
- **2026-10-14, the ambient meadow (96 bars, the tune in a third of the bars, no percussion, pad-only bars, piano
  fragments):** "did not like this ambient version; maybe a church or credits." Rule: **do not make a piece ambient by
  emptying it.** Sparseness read as a different place. The way to a piece one never tires of is section 2: longer, with an
  arc, the hook back late, the parts arriving, the middle voice doing the work; the energy and voices of v1 kept.

- **2026-10-14, the meadow by the Maple rules (96 bars, the hook said twice over harp and pad, the bass at 17, the
  middle-voice section, B doubled, the peak, the breakdown, the hook back at 73):** "good from around 2:27" (bar 73: the
  hook's return with everything, then the full second tune and the tail). "Together with your first version that can become
  something very good if you combine the ideas." Rule: **the slow build is not for Robin.** v1 is full from bar 5 and he
  liked it; the Maple's sixty thin bars before the return were the part he did not. Keep from the Maple: the
  breakdown-and-return, the bells doubling the flute on the return, the piano comp under it, the tail with the bells' turn
  and the harp's run. Keep from v1: its shape (intro, A, A', B up a tone, the bridge, A) and its density from bar 5.
  Section 2's "build to two thirds" is therefore softened: build within the first sixteen bars, keep the breakdown and
  the return as the one big event.
- **2026-10-14, Woodsmoke (D Mixolydian, a drone, a lyre in 3+3+2, a wooden flute, a hand drum, a wood block):** "pretty
  cool, but better closer to your first version." Rule: **the Viking colour goes in the tune and the mode, not in the
  instruments.** Keep the modal tune and a trace of the lilt; arrange it with v1's voices (flute, bells, harp, piano, pad,
  FM bass, shaker and rim) rather than drones and a lyre.
- **2026-10-14, the Ellinia recreation:** "you can clearly hear it is that song ... but everyone's playing their instrument
  a bit however they want, the tones are a bit all over the place; you can clearly hear the melody and what it's trying to
  do." Rule for the studies: the transcriptions are trustworthy for tune, chords and form, not for voicing; a recreation
  should assign voices by continuity (one lead line, the rest to the piano) and drop the stray notes.

- **2026-10-14, Woodsmoke v2 (the Mixolydian tune with v1's voices and shape):** "needs another melody; now it sounds
  like the meadows theme just with other instruments." Rule: **a second theme must differ in its rhythmic cell, its meter
  or its contour, not only in its notes and mode.** The same arrangement with a tune of quarters and eighths in 4/4 is
  the same theme to the ear. Woodsmoke v3 goes to six-eight (long-short-short, the lilt of oars), a different lead voice
  for the first tune (the clarinet), and a contour that starts low and climbs.

- **2026-10-14, Village, day (G major, 108, the clarinet's walking tune, the piano's off-beats as the engine, a sus
  before the cadences):** "the village is good." Rule confirmed: the guide's village row works (clarinet lead, the comp
  engine, the held sus, a key of its own); a new place gets a new key, a new lead voice and a new rhythmic cell.
- **2026-10-14, Woodsmoke v3 (six-eight, the clarinet low and climbing):** "bad, can be removed." All three Woodsmoke
  versions archived. What did not work, across the three: the Mixolydian tune itself never landed, in any clothes; the
  six-eight lilt did not help. Rule: when a tune fails in two arrangements, the tune is the problem; drop it rather than
  re-dress it a third time.

- **2026-10-14, Sea crossing (A Dorian, 84, two chords rocking, parallel maj7 for the lift, the harp as waves, a rowing
  drum under the second half):** "very good!" Rule confirmed: the biome doc's sea row and the guide together work;
  the Viking in the mode (Dorian) and in one drum, not in the instruments. One minute per crossing is right.

- **2026-10-14, The serpent passes (E Phrygian with the augmented second, 66, a heartbeat, a drone, the harp as
  bubbles):** "very good." Rule confirmed: mysterious and foreign is a mode first (the flat second, the augmented
  second), then slowness and size (a heartbeat, a drone, the pad with the fifth below); the biome doc's cave and
  cold rows can be trusted the same way.

- **2026-10-15, the first fast pieces (the fight and the raid charge):** measured, not yet heard. A fight piece with the same
  voices as a place piece needs about **twice the master level** (0.58 gave RMS -27, the quietest in the studio; 1.0 gave
  -22.6 with peak -6.6) because its parts are short and percussive. Rule: set the fight pieces to master 1.0 and the
  place pieces to 0.6, then measure.

- **2026-10-15, the title, the forest, the mountains, the night, the cave, the fight and the raid (the first round of
  one-minute place pieces), Robin's takes:**
  - Title: "I like it a lot; missing a memorable tune, so that you remember the song and start humming it." Forest: "starts
    off nice, then after 15 sec the tunes get weird, and later on too crazy, at around 31." Night: "I like it but it needs a
    theme tune." Mountains: "I like it a lot; switch the flute to a more Celtic thing (Grizzly Hills)." Cave: "very good."
    Fight and raid: "too much like some 80s retro cyber music; needs to be more heroic and Scandinavian; try something new."
  - Rule: **a piece Robin likes still needs a tune he can hum.** Two liked pieces (title, night) were missing it; a tune
    that is a chord outlined slowly (the title's) or a borrowed hook slowed (the night's) is not one. A hummable tune has
    one short rhythmic cell, a leap in it, and is said twice with a changed ending (section 2 already said so; it is now
    the first thing to check).
  - Rule: **a figure built from "the chord's root plus an interval" must be checked against the key.** The forest's bells
    took the raised fourth from each chord's root and so played a C sharp over G and a D sharp over Am7: the "weird" was
    chromatic notes, not the Lydian mode. Colour notes belong to the key, not to the chord of the moment.
  - Rule: **a climax is louder and fuller, not busier.** The forest's creature stacked a chord shimmer in eighths, a
    ten-note sweep every bar and a 1.5 swell: "too crazy". Keep one new line and one new event in a climax.
  - Rule: **"Scandinavian" and "heroic" come from the drums and the bowing, not the tempo.** The fight pieces at 140 and 150
    with a kick, snare, hats, a dsaw pulse and an eighth-note bass read as 80s retro. What reads as Nordic: a war drum
    (boom plus low tom) on 1 and 3, toms on the off-beats, shields knocking, bowed strings sawing open fifths on 1 and 3,
    a bass in half notes, a chant-like tune (repeated note, step, then a leap), a round horn (the lur), a fiddle. No drum
    machine samples (sn_dolf, the hats, bd_haus), no pulsing dsaw. Tempo around 116.
  - Rule confirmed: the Celtic colour Robin asked for on the mountains is in the voices (fiddle with late vibrato, a whistle
    with grace notes, a hand drum); the tune itself was liked and stays. This does not contradict "Viking in the tune, not
    the instruments": Robin asked for the instrument change himself, for a place that is not the Viking homeland.

- **2026-10-15, the third round (the v2s, the cave, Shield wall), Robin's takes:**
  - Title v2: "think is good". Meadow v2: "this is good"; make it longer "to not grow tired of it, and to also make you
    remember the theme tune". Night v2: "longer and some low variation, so that it feels relaxing and makes you want to
    retire to your home". Mountain v2: "sound good! Maybe longer and add some adventure background". Sea crossing: a
    happy, upbeat, adventurous version for the day; the crossing becomes the night's. Cave: "a bit too happy around 44
    seconds". Shield wall: "I like it from 1:14; it needs a quick upbeat to 1:14, then more drums and more action; excited
    and adventure, but not happy".
  - Rule confirmed: **a hummable tune is what makes Robin keep a piece.** The title and the night were both liked in v1
    and kept only once they had one.
  - Rule: **a long version is the theme coming back in new colours with something new between.** Robin's two aims for
    the long meadow are opposite pulls (never tire of it, but remember the tune); the answer is the hook four times in
    3:23, each in another voice (flute, flute and bells, clarinet, flute and bells), and between them sections that are
    new but keep the energy (a piano melody on its own chords, a minor cloud, a bells' bridge). Never a quiet stretch
    made by taking parts away.
  - Rule: **relaxing is the same tune in warmer, lower voices, not fewer notes.** For the night: the clarinet an octave
    down, a soft piano (the sound of indoors), the harp thinner; the tempo unchanged.
  - Rule: **"adventure" is motion under a broad tune.** The mountain's tune stays in half and whole notes at 64; the
    adventure is a frame drum in sixteenths with a 3+3+2 drive, low strings in eighths, a fiddle's second tune in eighths.
  - Rule: **a major chord in a dark place reads as happy.** The cave's "ore glowing" in F and Bb major was the moment
    Robin heard as too happy. Light in a dark piece comes from register and timbre (higher, brighter instruments) over
    minor, suspended or open chords.
  - Rule: **a fight starts where the action is.** Robin liked Shield wall from the point its biggest fill led back into
    the war drum and the chant; everything before that was too slow to get going (an 8-second ramp and a whole quieter
    first section). A fight piece: a ramp of a few seconds, drums from the first bar of the loop, a fill every four bars,
    and "not happy" means no relative-major chords (F and C in D minor); the minor's own VI and VII (Bb, C) with the
    raised seventh at the cadence are heroic enough.
  - Rule: **a day and a night version of one place can share a tune's contour.** The sea voyage keeps the crossing's
    shape (the fifth, the fourth, the third, down to the root and up) in major and twice as quick, so the two belong
    together; the meadow's night needed a tune of its own because its first version was only the day's hook slowed.

- **2026-10-15, the sea voyage and Shield wall v2, Robin's takes:** the sea voyage "sounds too much lullaby; it should be
  more like Wind Waker, like you are traversing water and going on adventure"; Shield wall's "flute sound in the
  beginning" should be "some other instrument, again, to make it less lullaby".
  - Rule: **the studio's soft voices together read as a lullaby.** The flute (a sine), the harp in arpeggios, the bells,
    the pad and a skipping dotted tune are the night's palette; a happy tempo does not undo them. Adventure is a brass
    lead on a broad tune with big leaps and long notes, strings doubling it, and an accompaniment that never stops
    running (a plucked gallop, a bouncing bass, timpani and rolls).
  - Rule: **a triangle wave reads as a flute.** The lur (triangle, a thread of saw, a sine below) was heard as a flute;
    a horn has to be built on saws through a low-pass. The mountain's horn is the studio's brass; for a fight, a quicker
    attack and a rougher second saw.
  - Rule kept: the Viking and the place are in the mode and the tune; but Robin's references (Wind Waker, Grizzly Hills)
    are about the instruments and the motion, so when he names a game, the arrangement follows it.

- **2026-10-15, Robin's brief for the raid music (Shield wall from scratch, three to compare):** "lots of drums; going up
  quick the first 5 seconds to become intense and adventurous; it starts playing once you decide to charge a village to
  raid; epic and exciting; thrilling." So: a ramp of about five seconds played once (from one drum to everything), then a
  loop that is intense from its first bar; drums in every bar (real drums and wood only); the studio's fight kit in
  `music/_fight.rb`. Three answers to compare, each different in rhythm and mood, not only in notes: A dark and driving
  (Phrygian, 140, 3+3+2), B the heroic gallop (6/8, Dorian), C big and broad (half-time with the slam on 3, E minor, 96).

- **2026-10-15, Shield wall A, B and C:** "I don't like either of those 3. I don't like the lyre for fighting." Rule:
  **no lyre (a plucked string) in fight music.** It was the running engine in Shield wall v2, v3, A, B and C, and the
  one raid passage Robin liked (v1 from 1:14) is the one with no lyre. The drive in a fight comes from drums (a low drum
  played soft and fast, `fk_rumble`, the toms, the frame drum) under sustained strings and the horn, not from a plucked
  or a short repeated synth note (the second is the "80s retro" pulse). A2, B2 and C2 test whether the lyre was the
  whole problem.

## 8. The next meadow piece, by these rules
