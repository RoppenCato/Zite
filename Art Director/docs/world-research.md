# World maps that read as places: research notes

Mitch, AD, 2026-11-02, on Robin's request. **How far this goes:** web searches turned up few real analyses of why these maps work. What follows is what those sources state, plus my own reading of Robin's Dokapon picture and of the games' well-known maps from memory. The conclusions are my judgement, not established findings.

## What the sources say

- **Dokapon Kingdom** is one very large board with several continents, each with its own castle and usually shops and dungeons. Regions open one by one as the story moves on, and its routes form many loops, so one spin offers several choices. ([Dokapon wiki, continents](https://dokapon.fandom.com/wiki/Continent); [Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/17274/dokapon-kingdom-wii); [RPGamer](https://archive.rpgamer.com/event/rtts2008/firemyst/dokaponkingdom-imp.html); [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/hands-on-dokapon-kingdom-stings-dorm-room-rpg/))
- **Super Mario Bros. 3** gives each world its own theme and map music. Water Land is a chain of islands with changing paths, and Pipe Land is islands joined by pipes. ([Super Mario Wiki, Water Land](https://www.mariowiki.com/World_3_Map_(Water_Land)); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros._3))
- **Super Mario World** tried early map layouts before its final one, and prerelease data suggests a reworked terrain with a continuous loop of path. ([The Cutting Room Floor](https://tcrf.net/Prerelease:Super_Mario_World_(SNES)/Background_Graphics_and_Tilemaps))
- **Mario Party** boards let the theme drive the layout, and a fan review of a board faulted a good theme let down by a confusing path layout. Treat that as an opinion, not design documentation. ([Mario Wiki, Mystery Land](https://mario.fandom.com/wiki/Mystery_Land); [Mario Party Legacy](https://mariopartylegacy.com/2016/10/create-a-mario-party-board-contest-1-results/))

## What I read in Robin's Dokapon picture

1. **Each region is one big readable mass of ground.** Dark forest, tan desert, white snow, each with a clear edge. You can tell them apart when the map is very small.
2. **Land has a shape.** Coast all round, bays, small islands. It never looks like a rectangle.
3. **Bridges sit where land nearly meets,** so a join between regions is a place you notice.
4. **Its weakness is ours too:** its roads run on a square grid. That is the pattern Robin dislikes, so I did not take that part. Our roads wander.

## What I took from it for Zite

| Principle | In the concept |
| --- | --- |
| A region reads at thumbnail size | Green meadows, dark forest mass, golden patchwork |
| Hard joins become places | King Goo's stone bridge, the mountain wall and Grumblebark's pass |
| A place has its own ground | Puddlewick's paving and wall, Tumbletree's clearing, Goldencomb's yard |
| Spots belong to a place | The road spots near Puddlewick sit on its paving, inside its wall |
| Things stand between regions | Sea, strait, river and mountains, so no two grounds meet in a straight line |
| Roads wander but stay readable | Curved roads through clearings, no crossings, checked by the tool |
| Keep it simple to read | Symbols on plates, wild spots recede (the Mario Party review's warning) |
