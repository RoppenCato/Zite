# The classes and how abilities are earned

Miles, GD, 2026-11-02. **A proposal, all of it mine until Robin says otherwise.** Robin asked: "Lay some groundwork for what they
do, what makes them different, what abilities they have... We need to make the classes feel good and different to play" and "Should
the player have a skill tree and put points when leveling up? Whats the best system for this mobile game?"

The brief already says: classes have basic abilities that fit their style; "more skills and higher ranks are bought, found or
earned"; abilities should be "fun and make the classes feel different and exciting, not only bigger numbers" (Robin, 2026-10-31).
Every number here is a first setting, to be played and changed.

## What is wrong today

I read the rules and the data. The classes differ less than their names promise.

1. **The meters do nothing at the start.** Each class has a good idea: Valor builds when hit, Mischief when hitting, Arcana each round,
   Aim when a blow misses you. But no class starts with an ability that spends its meter. The pips fill and mean nothing until a
   random offer happens to bring a spender.
2. **Abilities come by luck, and rarely.** A pick comes every 4 levels (4, 8, 12...), between two random abilities, plus random
   lessons at shrines. You can't plan a character, and you can't see what is coming. Two Knights differ only by luck.
3. **The classes share tricks.** Losing a turn is on the Knight (Shield Bonk), the Archer (Net Toss) and the Wizard (Pocket Watch).
   A barrier is on the Knight (Castle Stance) and the Wizard (Bubble Shield, Moonlight Veil). Most of the rest are "a hit of N power".
4. **The numbers grow the same.** All four gain 2 attack and 1 defense a level. The Wizard has the highest attack (14), though the
   brief calls the Knight the high damage one, and a Wizard's plain attack is as good as a spell's base.
5. **The brief's Wizard isn't special.** "Charges for a turn, then a huge hit": any class can Charge, and it doubles the next hit, ability or not. Nothing makes
   it the Wizard's way.

## What makes a class feel different

One rule for all four: **each class asks a different question every round.** Not bigger numbers: a different thing to think about.

| | Knight | Trickster | Wizard | Archer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The fantasy** | The wall that hits back | The cheat who gets away with it | The one who plans three rounds ahead | The hunter who reads the prey |
| **The question** | "Which blow do I take, and when do I make them pay?" | "Cash in now, or risk one more hit?" | "Can I survive until it lands?" | "What is it about to do, and how do I punish it?" |
| **Meter builds by** | Being hit | Hitting | Time | Blows missing you, and crits |
| **Best guard** | Block, Counter | Dodge | Brace, for mana | Dodge, or whichever the `next:` line calls for |
| **Weak at** | Slow to kill, little mana | Fragile if the chain breaks | Thin, and helpless when out of mana | Small hits against hard shells |
| **Round feels like** | Slow, heavy, one big payoff | Quick, greedy, many small numbers | Quiet, then a screen-filling hit | Calm and exact, always a step ahead |

### One always-on rule each (new)

Short enough to read on the class card, and felt from the first fight.

- **Knight, Stalwart:** a Block stops more (60% instead of 50%), and a Counter always builds 1 Valor.
- **Trickster, On a Roll:** each hit in a row raises crit chance a little, until you are hit. The chain the brief asks for.
- **Wizard, Focus:** Charge is better for the Wizard: it triples the next spell instead of doubling it, and gives back a little mana. The brief's Wizard, from the first fight.
- **Archer, First Shot:** strikes first, as now, and that first arrow can't be weak: it counts as charged.

## How abilities are earned: my recommendation

**A small skill board with points. Not a big tree, and not random picks.**

### The options

| | Good | Bad on a phone game like this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Random picks** (today) | Simple, a little surprise | You can't plan, and characters are decided by luck |
| **Big branching tree** | Deep, a long goal | Doesn't fit a phone screen, one wrong choice can spoil a character, huge to balance |
| **Fixed unlocks by level** | Nothing to get wrong | No choices: every Knight is the same |
| **Only bought or found** | Rewards exploring | Bad luck can leave you weak, and nothing to look forward to on a level-up |
| **A small board with points** (my pick) | You choose, you see what is coming, every level gives something | A new screen to build |

### How it would work

- **One screen for the class:** three short lines of abilities, each line a way to play (Knight: Shield, Banner, Valor), the 8 abilities on it, always
  visible. You see what is ahead, even before you can have it. Mitch, AD would design it.
- **A point on every level to 10, then every second level** (19 by level 30, against 22 to fill the board). Early levels always give something, and late on you
  have to choose: **you can't have everything**, so two Knights differ by choice.
- **A point learns an ability or raises a rank.** Stronger abilities need a level: first row from level 1, second from 4, third from 8.
- **Carry four into a fight.** The abilities menu shows four buttons without paging. Choosing which four is half the fun, and it stays thumb-friendly.
- **Free to change in any town.** Points and the four can be reset at no cost. Nobody can ruin a character, and trying things is the game.

### Bought, found and earned, as the brief says

- **Earned:** points from levels. Beating a region's boss for the first time gives one extra point: a boss should feel like a turning point.
- **Found:** a rare **Tome** in chests, and sometimes at shrines, gives one extra point. Shrines stop teaching random abilities.
- **Bought:** each town sells one dear Tome, once. This also gives gold something big to go on.

## What it would take

1. **Rules:** the new abilities and always-on rules, the changed stats, points, carrying four, free reset. All data plus a few systems, with tests. A save
   migration gives existing characters their points back to spend.
2. **A balance run:** the pane bot plays each class through fights, measuring how long they last and who wins. So no class is left hopeless. Numbers are still yours to feel.
3. **Screens:** Mitch, AD's board design, then the screen, then the four-carried choice in the fight menu.
4. **Feel:** each class's hits, sounds and pips, with Mitch, AD and Nigel, SD, once the rules are right.

Today's characters are not lost at any step.
